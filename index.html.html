About the Project

The Prosopographical Database of Gasmouloi (PDG) is a digital humanities initiative dedicated to documenting and analysing individuals of mixed Roman and Latin descent who lived during the Late Byzantine period (thirteenth to fifteenth centuries). This web-based platform combines a prosopographical methodology with database technology to create a comprehensive, accessible research tool.

The PDG was developed alongside the doctoral thesis 'Mobility, Integration and Identity in Late Byzantium: the case of gasmouloi' (Royal Holloway, University of London, 2026), and serves as a supplementary digital resource. Following the model of the Prosopographisches Lexikon der Palaiologenzeit, each record treats a single individual, bringing together name forms in Greek and Latin, dates, sphere of activity, kinship ties, and the primary and secondary sources in which they appear.

The PDG pursues three core objectives:

  1. Documentation: to create a comprehensive prosopographical database of gasmouloi, recording biographical details, spheres of activity, kinship networks and source citations for every identifiable individual.
  2. Analysis: to enable quantitative and qualitative analysis of gasmouloi as a social group, making visible patterns of family, office and geography that are difficult to discern from narrative sources read in isolation.
  3. Accessibility: to make this research freely available to the public and scholarly community through an intuitive, web-based interface that requires no specialised technical training to use.

Guidance on the scope of the project, its editorial and citation conventions, and instructions for searching, filtering and visualising the records is given on the Guide page.

Guide

What the page shows

The Database page lists every record in the PDG. The filters at the top narrow the list; the list itself updates as you type, so there is no need to press Search unless you prefer to.

Record Cards

  • A coloured stripe down the left edge gives the family: purple for the Frangopouloi and amber for the Orsini.
  • The entry code sits above the name
  • The dates beneath the name are the earliest and latest years at which the person is attested. See Dating below.
  • The sphere tags are clickable and filters the whole list to that sphere.
  • + Add to Graphics places the individual in the selection used by the Graphics page; View Profile opens the full record.

Entry codes

Every record carries a permanent identifier of the form F-001, with a one-letter family prefix and a three-digit number.

F
Frangopouloi, including the women recorded under the feminine form Frangopoulina.
O
Orsini, including the Epirote branch filed under Komnēnos Angelos Doukas.

Numbers are currently assigned in order of earliest attestation and are used in the Family & relations field to point from one record to another.

Searching

The search box runs over the whole record: entry code, all name forms, the sphere, the kinship field, the biography, and the primary, secondary and manuscript citations.

Several words
Terms are combined with AND and order does not matter, so frangopoulos georgios and georgios frangopoulos return the same men.
Phrases
Double quotation marks keep a phrase whole, which is the way to search a title or a citation: "Actes de Lavra".
Accents
Diacritics are ignored, so Γεωργιος finds Γεώργιος, and Komnene finds Komnēnē. An accented query still works. Final sigma and medial sigma are treated as the same letter, so a name typed in isolation is found in running text.
Family names
A search for a family returns everyone in it, including members filed under another surname. Searching a form that only some members bore returns only those members.
Example. Orsini returns the Epirote branch, though they are filed under Komnēnos Angelos Doukas; Komnenos Angelos Doukas does not return Richard or John I. Likewise Frangopoulos and Frangopouloi return the women recorded as Frangopoulina, while Frangopoulina does not return the men.

Dating

The two date fields hold the earliest and latest years at which an individual is attested. They are not necessarily birth and death years. Where a birth or death is recorded it occupies the appropriate field, but for most individuals the dates mark the span of the documentation rather than the span of a life.

1330 — 1368
Attested across a period: two different dates are recorded.
1330
Attested at a single moment: one date is recorded.
Undated
No date is recorded.

The date filter

The slider covers 1150 to 1500 and its two handles set a window; the handles cannot be brought closer than ten years. A record appears when its span overlaps the window at any point, rather than when it falls wholly inside it, so a man attested from 1290 to 1340 is returned by a window of 1300–1310.

Undated records are treated as spanning the whole range and so are never hidden by the date filter.

Sorting, spheres and selection

Sphere
The dropdown is built from the values present in the records, so it reflects the current state of the database rather than a fixed list. Spheres can also be typed into the search box; the dropdown is the way to filter to one exactly.
Sort By
The default order is by name. The four date orders sort by the earliest or the latest recorded year, ascending or descending; undated records are placed at the end in every case.
Select All
Adds every record in the current result set to the graphics selection, not the whole database The button becomes Deselect All once everything shown is selected.
Reset
Clears the search, the sphere, the sort order and the date range. It does not clear the graphics selection.

The selection follows you between pages and survives a reload, but it belongs to the browser session: closing the tab discards it.

Spheres of activity

A sphere records the field in which an individual is attested as acting. It describes what the sources show a person doing, not what they may be presumed to have done, and an individual may carry more than one.

Military
Service under arms, whether on campaign, in garrison or at sea. This is the sphere in which gasmouloi are most often met as a body, and it covers command as well as the ranks.
Political
Office and administration in the service of the Roman state or a Latin lordship, including provincial governorship, service in the imperial household, and the conduct of diplomacy and embassies.
Religious
Position within the Church, from the episcopate to the parish clergy and the monastic houses, together with those attested acting in an ecclesiastical cause or before a church court.
Cultural
Learning, letters and the crafts: scribes, painters, teachers, and those attested through the books they copied, owned or annotated.
Economic
Land, trade and property: those attested as holding, working, leasing or disputing an estate, and those met in the course of commerce.

Building a selection

The Graphics page visualises the selected group of individuals. Nothing is drawn until individuals have been added from the Database page with + Add to Graphics, singly or by filtering and using Select All.

Two views are available: a timeline of the selected individuals, and a distribution by sphere.

Timeline

The axis runs from 1150 to 1500, labelled every fifty years and ruled every twenty-five.

Bar
Where two different dates are recorded, and the bar runs between them.
Point
Where one date is recorded a single point is added.
Colour
Frangopouloi   Orsini  

Hovering a marker shows the years it represents and every name is a link to their full profile.

Undated individuals cannot be placed on the timeline, a note beside the sort control reports how many of the selected group are missing from the drawing.

Sorting the timeline

Date
One continuous sequence, earliest or latest first, ordered by the earliest recorded year and then by the latest.
Name
By surname, then forename. Unnamed individuals collect at the end rather than sorting to the front on an empty name field.
Family
Grouped under a heading for each family, with the family's colour and a count, and ordered by date within each group.
Sphere
Grouped by sphere. Unspecified groups are placed at the bottom.

Distribution by sphere

The bar shows the proportion of the selection falling under each sphere, and the cards beneath it name the individuals in each, largest sphere first. Every name links to its profile.

The record in full

A profile is the record for one individual with a heading, a biography, attested forms, relations and citations. Profiles are reached from View Profile on a record card, and from any name on the Graphics page. Apparatus are only displayed when something is recorded.

Heading

  • The entry code, in the family's colour.
  • The name
  • The dates of attestation and the spheres.

The Apparatus

Biography
A short account of what the sources record.
Attested forms
The Greek headword first, then the Latin form, then any other recorded spellings.
Family & relations
Kinship and documented association, one relation per line. Cross-references give the entry code of the individual concerned, so the relation can be followed by searching that code.
Primary sources
The documents and narrative sources in which the individual appears, cited by short title.
Manuscripts
Manuscript witnesses, where the individual is attested in one.
Secondary sources
Modern scholarship on the individual, cited by short title. A reference to the accompanying thesis, with page numbers, indicates where they are discussed there.

Full bibliographical entries of short titles are given on the Bibliography page, to which every profile links.

Short titles

Records cite their sources by short title, the Bibliography page offers the full citation.

Finding a reference

Search
Runs over the short title, the full reference and the note, so a work can be found by author, by title, or by the abbreviation used for it.
Categories
Primary sources; Secondary literature; and All which shows both.
Order
Alphabetical by short title, grouped under an initial letter, and insensitive to accents so that a Greek or accented title files where it is expected.

Greek is displayed in a font chosen for its coverage of the polytonic range, so headwords and titles render consistently whatever the machine.
